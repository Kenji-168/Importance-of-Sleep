Why sleep matters.

Sleep is not simply time away from activity. It supports your physical health, mental well-being, and daily functioning.

✦✦✧
◌
Everyday functioning

Sleep supports attention, memory, and mood.

♡
Health over time

Healthy sleep is connected to physical and heart health.

Think of sleep as a basic health need—not an optional reward after everything else.

02 / THE FUNDAMENTALS02 / 10

Hours matter. Quality does too.

Sleep duration and sleep quality are related, but they are not the same thing.

CDC recommendation
7+ hours

Adults ages 18–60: at least 7 hours per night.

8–10 hours for ages 13–17
Sleep quality
Refreshing · Less interrupted

Enough time in bed ≠ refreshing sleep

Repeated interruptions may leave a person tired even after spending enough time in bed.

The recommendations describe age groups. They do not guarantee identical needs or outcomes for every person.

05 / YOUR BODY05 / 10

Sleep and heart health.

The CDC describes links between sleep problems and cardiovascular health over time.

♥

Sleep apnea

Associated with higher risk of high blood pressure, heart attack, and stroke.

Insomnia

Linked to high blood pressure and heart disease.

Words matter: “Linked to” or “associated with” does not mean one poor night inevitably causes heart disease.

06 / WHEN SLEEP FALLS SHORT06 / 10

Not all sleep loss looks alike.

Sleep deprivation is not getting enough sleep. Sleep deficiency is a broader problem.

Sleep deprivation

Too little sleep

Not getting enough sleep.

Sleep deficiency

Beyond hours alone

May include poor quality or sleep at the wrong time.

◎Focus & learning

Can be impaired

↗Reactions

Can slow down

⚠Safety

Injury risk can increase

Long-term deficiency is linked with chronic health problems; individual outcomes vary. Do not drive while sleepy.

07 / WHAT YOU CAN DO07 / 10

Small habits, better rest.

Healthy sleep habits focus on regularity, your surroundings, and what you do before bed.

01

Keep a routine

Use consistent wake times and a relaxing wind-down.

02

Set the scene

Aim for a cool, quiet room; reduce bright light and devices before bed.

03

Notice evening choices

Late caffeine, large meals near bedtime, and alcohol can interfere with sleep.

Try one or two realistic changes. Habits do not replace professional care for persistent sleep problems.

08 / WHEN TO ASK FOR HELP08 / 10

Recognize the warning signs.

Sleep apnea is one example of a disorder that can disturb sleep quality.

01

During sleep

Loud snoring, breathing that starts and stops, or gasping for air.

02

During the day

Sleepiness, fatigue, or difficulty focusing may occur.

03

What to do

Discuss warning signs with a healthcare professional; assessment may include a sleep study.

Symptoms alone do not prove sleep apnea. This slide does not diagnose any sleep disorder.
